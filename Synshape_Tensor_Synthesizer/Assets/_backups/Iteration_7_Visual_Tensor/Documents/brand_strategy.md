# Synshape — Brand and positioning decision

## The chosen name

**Synshape** is the recommended product name. It is a compact portmanteau of **synthesis** and **shape**: it names the distinctive act at the centre of the product, namely turning human spatial input into a typed computational shape. The name is easy to pronounce in English and German, is not tied to a specific runtime, and gives the visual system an immediate direction: axes, surfaces, folds, and resolved form.

> **Synshape is the gesture-native tensor authoring instrument for turning spatial ideas into locally valid ONNX graphs.**

The preliminary digital screening did not surface an obvious AI-software product called Synshape; by contrast, directions such as **Tensora** and **Tensorium** returned existing AI/software businesses, and **Tensym** is associated with the IEEE TENSYMP symposium. This is a directional marketing screen, not trademark clearance. Before commercial use, the owner should commission trademark and domain checks in the intended jurisdictions and classes.

| Criterion                        | Synshape assessment | Why it matters                                                                                             |
| -------------------------------- | ------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Mechanism fit**                | High                | It combines “synthesis” with “shape”, the product’s direct transformation.                                 |
| **Category flexibility**         | High                | It can extend from an instrument to formats, validators, and collaboration without being locked to ONNX.   |
| **Technical tone**               | High                | It reads as a modern creative-developer product without using generic “AI”, “tensor”, or “graph” language. |
| **Recall and pronunciation**     | High                | Two short, distinct syllables work in spoken demos and developer conversation.                             |
| **Preliminary collision signal** | Favorable           | No direct product collision surfaced in the exploratory screen; legal clearance remains required.          |

## Defensible category position

Synshape should define a new, precise category: **gesture-native tensor authoring**. Existing ONNX tools visibly cover construction, deployment, optimization, and visual inspection; the official ONNX tool directory places Netron, VisualDL, and Zetane in visualization, while Netron calls itself a model viewer. Synshape is not a viewer bolted onto a finished model. It is an **authoring pre-compiler**: a user draws an intent, sees shape semantics and route legality, then serializes an interoperable artifact.

| Product question                      | Synshape answer                                               | User benefit                                                           |
| ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| How does a model begin?               | With a line, surface, volume, or nD gesture.                  | The blank graph becomes an expressive spatial workspace.               |
| When is an operation explained?       | Before export, at the point of connection.                    | Shape conflict becomes visible before it turns into a runtime failure. |
| Where does model construction happen? | Entirely in the browser, with no application backend.         | Low latency, local control, and a usable offline-first foundation.     |
| What leaves the workspace?            | A raw ONNX protobuf, preset, or optional initializer package. | The result is portable, rather than trapped inside the tool.           |

## Unique selling proposition

The most supportable USP is not “the first visual ONNX tool.” It is:

> **The first authoring experience in its category to make pointer telemetry itself part of tensor synthesis, validating the graph before it becomes a model file.**

This formulation puts the product mechanism first: gesture-to-tensor resolution, shape-aware operation inference, local-first graph compilation, and raw ONNX serialization. It avoids the broad and hard-to-prove claim that no other visual ONNX tool exists.

## Brand system

The **Synshape mark** is a folded four-axis tensor knot: graphite planes fold around one Tensor-Lime core, with a cyan structural edge. In the UI the mark is symbol-only; the precisely rendered wordmark is typeset in Space Grotesk rather than generated inside an image. The mark has three operational roles: the 28–38 px navigation mark, the large 88 px intro mark, and a 20 px favicon/compact mode mark.

The typography remains **Space Grotesk** for authored/product language and **IBM Plex Mono** for measured machine state. **Tensor Lime `#C8FF5A`** remains exclusive to validity, active progression, and the mark’s core. Cyan denotes topology and data structure; coral communicates mathematical invalidity.

## Launch messaging

The landing sentence should be: **“Draw a surface. Resolve the graph.”**

The support sentence should be: **“Synshape turns pointer telemetry into typed tensor structures and exports an inspectable ONNX graph — locally.”**

The product avoids generic transformation rhetoric. It should speak in material verbs such as _draw, resolve, route, inspect,_ and _serialize_.
